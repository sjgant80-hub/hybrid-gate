What this is

This is Rung 3 of a ladder of tests toward computing by geometry (small networks that read by settling into stored patterns, with no trained model) instead of by large models. Rungs 1 and 2 built a cell that reads a digit by settling and a loop that improves it with no model. Rung 3 asks how such a cell should sit inside a real job: let it do the fuzzy part, let exact code do the exact part, and keep the decision in a few lines a person can read and audit.

The job is reading handwritten account numbers that end in a Luhn check digit, the same kind of check used on card and account numbers. A single misread digit always breaks the check, so exact code can catch it, and sometimes repair it. What the other rungs found, held and failed, is on the ladder page.

Words on this page

Cell, settleThe small digit reader from Rungs 1 and 2. It reads a digit by flipping its bits towards stored patterns until nothing more changes, and answers with the class it ends up nearest.
Luhn check digitThe last digit of the number is computed from the others by a fixed rule, as on card numbers. Any single wrong digit breaks the rule, so a misread always shows.
Confidence, τA digit's confidence is its top class's share of the total fit after settling. τ is the share a digit must reach to be trusted, chosen on the tune numbers only, before the test numbers were read.
Accept, referAccept: the number is passed on as read. Refer: it goes to a person, with the reason (no fit, low confidence, or check failed).
Margin gateAn extra gate tried after tuning, as an exploration: it judges a digit's confidence by its margin before settling. It is shown beside the result and is not one of the sealed claims.
Repair, doubtful digitIf the check fails, the doubtful digit (the least confident one) is solved from the check rule. The fix is accepted only if the solved value was the cell's own second choice for that digit and every other digit is confident.
Step-codeThe exact part: ordinary tested code (the Luhn rule), checked here on all 100,000 five-digit strings.
The gateThe few lines that make the accept or refer decision, shown in full on this page so a person can audit them.

How it works

Step by step

  1. Assemble. Each held-out number is four handwritten test digits plus a fifth whose label is their Luhn check digit. Every image is used once.
  2. Read. The settling cell reads each digit and reports how much of its fit the winning class holds: its confidence.
  3. Check. Exact code computes the Luhn check. Any single misread digit breaks it, always.
  4. Repair. If the check fails, the code takes the least confident digit and solves for the one value that would fix the check. If that value is the cell's own runner-up for that digit, the number is accepted as repaired.
  5. Decide. The gate accepts a number only when the check holds and every digit is confident enough (τ), or after a repair; otherwise it refers it, with the reason.

    Adoption

    For a business

    Keying handwritten account numbers is real work. A small owned reader can take the clear cases, and exact arithmetic catches every single misread digit. Everything doubtful goes to a person, with a reason.

    For engineering

    Put the fuzzy part in the cell, the exact part in tested code, and the decision in a function short enough to audit. The step-code is checked on all 100,000 five-digit strings.

    For risk and procurement

    No model, no vendor, no network. Every decision is logged with its reason, and the record re-derives from pinned files on GitHub's runner and in your browser.

    A pilot, step by step

    1. Pick one keying task where the input carries its own check (a check digit, a total, a reference that must exist).
    2. Write down the bars it must meet before anything is built, and seal them: how often an accepted case may be wrong, and how much it must take off your team.
    3. Tune on your past cases only; keep a held-out slice untouched.
    4. Freeze it, then judge it once on the held-out slice. The fee is due only if it beat your current way.
    5. Run it beside your keyers, deciding nothing, and compare every accept with what they keyed.

    What it runs on: Node.js 20 or a current browser. No GPU, no cloud service, no model download.

    White paper: a hybrid with an inspectable gate

    The question. A settling cell is good at fuzzy matching and bad at exact work; code is the reverse. Rung 3 asks whether combining them, with the decision kept in readable code, gives a reader you can trust on a real job: high precision on what it accepts, and a reason for everything it refers. The answer, below, is mixed: every decision carried its reason and wrong accepts fell sharply, but accepted numbers were not right often enough to meet the bar.

    The method. MNIST handwritten digits, converted the way the optical digits were made. A cell grown by Rung 1's kernel with Rung 1's settings on half the training digits. Tune numbers from the other half; held-out numbers from the 10,000 test digits, read once after the cell and τ were frozen.

    The results, from the record:

      The limits, plainly:

      • Numbers are assembled from isolated digits, not cut from real forms. Finding digits on a page is a separate step.
      • The digit reader is the ceiling. At about 85% per digit, five-digit numbers carry too many double errors for 99% precision, whatever the gate does.
      • The sealed τ rule had no answer on tune; the gap was filled by a disclosed amendment before the test digits were read. The margin gate shown beside is exploratory, designed after tuning, and not a claim.
      • Rung 3 is not a mesh (Rung 4), not a defence (Rung 5), and not on a chip (Rung 7).
